How Load Lab works

1 · Paste the email. Freight lines straight from the customer, any format. An AI reader extracts the freight; a pattern reader takes over if it's unavailable. Nothing you paste is stored.

2 · Check the freight. Amber boxes are guesses — the email didn't say, so fix them before quoting. Toggles: stack = other freight may sit on top of it · tip = may lie on its side · drop = delivery order on multi-drop jobs (drop 1 loads last, by the doors).

3 · How is it loaded? The freight decides what's realistic: methods that can't work are crossed out (nobody handballs a 380 kg pallet), and the likely one is pre-selected. Forklift + long heavy pieces means side-loading through a curtain-sider — curtain bodies run Luton to Artic.

The verdict. The smallest vehicle the load genuinely fits, named with the body spec your DM logs use (Luton Curtain, 7.5T Tail). The packer is strict: walls are walls — a 121 cm pallet does not go in a 120 cm gap, and no leeway is ever added. Sizes are the Cal North fleet's internal load space; payloads and pallet ratings come from the vehicle guide (table under step 3).

The loading model. Pallets and boxes floor-load in rows front to back — several layouts are tried and the best wins. Pallets double-stack on stackable pallet tops when the floor runs out (always flagged for confirmation). Boxes only ever sit on stackable freight. Lengths lie on the floor down the side or in the rear gap, and ride on top only where stackable freight fully supports them — nothing floats, nothing loads diagonally.

The stats. LDM = loading metres, the floor length the load occupies. Weight aft = how far back the load's centre of gravity sits — high numbers mean brief the driver to load heavy freight at the bulkhead.

Trust rule. If it says FITS, a driver can load it that way. A tight hand-load might squeeze into less — but it will never suggest a vehicle the freight can't physically enter.

Fleet loading knowledge — tail lifts, curtains, handball limits
VehicleTail liftPlatformBody options
Handball: ~25 kg one person, ~50 kg two people (HSE guideline figures, good conditions). Forklift through the back doors: pieces up to ~2 m; extended forks stretch that to ~2.4 m; longer than that is a side-load through the curtain — curtain-side bodies exist from Luton up to Artic, nothing smaller. Tail-lift jobs: hard level ground, freight on a pump-truckable pallet, and it must fit on the platform — long lengths can't ride a tail lift. Typical ratings from UK hire fleets and TPN's vehicle & tail-lift delivery guide; check the actual vehicle when it matters.
